# Directions and style starting points

Reviewed: 2026-11-08

Use this to make directions really differ. These are starting points, not a ceiling or a menu: mix them, push past them, or invent something better. The project's brand and existing system always come first.

## Axes

Two directions should differ on at least two of these. If they differ only in color, they are one direction.

| Axis | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Density | Airy, one idea per screen | Dense, many items to scan and compare |
| Temperature | Cool, neutral, technical | Warm, human, crafted |
| Material | Flat and digital | Tactile: paper, grain, depth, real light |
| Type voice | Quiet sans, invisible | Expressive serif or display, part of the identity |
| Layout | Grid-locked and symmetric | Editorial, asymmetric, art-directed |
| Motion energy | Still, state changes only | Choreographed, a felt part of the product |

## Starting points

**Quiet operations.** For dashboards, admin, queues, compliance: repeated scanning and action. Avoid for consumer branding or storytelling. Looks cheap when it turns into gray on gray, decorative cards, low-contrast status colors. Pairs with clear SaaS. Conflicts with cinematic and playful. Look at Carbon, Atlassian, Primer.

**Clear SaaS.** For onboarding, settings, pricing, account flows. Avoid inside dense back-office tools, where polish slows scanning. Looks cheap with oversized heroes inside the app, pastel gradients, inconsistent empty-state art. Pairs with quiet operations and editorial data. Look at Nicelydone, SaaSFrame, Polaris.

**Editorial data.** For reports, analytics stories, market and benchmark pages: evidence that must read well. Avoid for bulk editing or fast entry. Looks cheap with decorative charts, vague callouts, animated counters, weak axis labels. Pairs with quiet operations. Look at strong newsroom data pages and Refactoring UI's hierarchy rules.

**Crafted and tactile.** For products about physical craft: print, food, fashion, objects, places. Paper, grain, ink, real photography, a serif with character. Avoid for tools where texture slows reading. Looks cheap with stock textures, fake paper filters, low-resolution photos, decoration that hides the product. Pairs with editorial. Conflicts with quiet operations. Look at the craft's own artifacts first, then galleries.

**Playful and touchable.** For learning, creator tools, casual products: warmth and touch feedback. Avoid for money, security, medical, or compliance flows. Looks cheap with toy overload, too much bounce, weak disabled states. Conflicts with quiet operations and cinematic. Look at Material 3 motion and product galleries.

**Cinematic product.** For a hero product, venue, or launch: one memorable first view and guided inspection. Avoid where users repeat tasks or compare data. Looks cheap with a dark generic mood image, a blurred product, gradient-only heroes, motion that hides content. Conflicts with quiet operations and game HUD. Look at Godly, Awwwards.

**Scroll story.** For complex features, methodology, case studies: paced understanding. Avoid where users need random access or quick edits. Looks cheap with hijacked scrolling, parallax gimmicks, hidden content, broken keyboard paths. Conflicts with quiet operations. Look at Osmo, Codrops.

**Readable game HUD.** For HUDs, menus, inventories, feedback under load. Avoid for standard workflows without play pressure. Looks cheap with illegible fantasy chrome, particle noise, copied game art, color-only status. Conflicts with clear SaaS and editorial data. Look at Game UI Database, Interface In Game.

**Minimal utility.** For small tools and single-purpose pages: one job, nothing extra. Avoid when the product needs to build trust or brand. Looks cheap when "minimal" means unfinished: default fonts, no hierarchy, no states. Pairs with almost anything as a secondary. Look at One Page Love, Minimal Gallery.

## Mixing

A strong direction is often a mix. Name which leads and what each adds, with rough weights, for example `crafted 60, editorial 30, clear SaaS 10`. Check the conflicts above: a mix of two conflicting starting points needs a reason.
